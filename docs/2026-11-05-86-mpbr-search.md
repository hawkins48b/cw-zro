# MPBR search without the 200 unit limit

- **Date:** 2026-11-05
- **Branch:** task/86-mpbr-search
- **Issue:** [#86](https://github.com/hawkins48b/cw-zro/issues/86)
- **Status:** Delivered with known issues, on `develop`, not in production yet

## Summary

`calculateMPBR` searched zeros from 1 to 200 with a 5 unit step and a 900 unit range. Large targets were under-reported, and the reported zero jumped between the near and far crossings.

**Delivered:** the search now solves for the best arc directly. It has no limit on the zero and no 900 unit limit, and the zero is always the far crossing.

Results on the .308 test profile, in yd:

| Target | Before | After | Brute force, 0.05 yd steps |
|--------|--------|-------|----------------------------|
| 8 in | 290 | 293.4 | 294.00 |
| 20 in | 415 | 424.3 | 424.39 |
| 30 in | 475 | 498.9 | 499.13 |
| 300 in | 900 (capped) | 1144.2 | 1144.89 |

**Tolerance:** `mpbrMax` is at most 1 unit under the best any zero can give, and at most 0.1 over it. The largest gap measured was 0.69 yd. Imperial and metric agree within 1 unit: the largest difference was 0.82 yd, over 400 combinations.

## Practical change: "Set MPBR" saves the far zero

"Set MPBR for current profile" now saves the far crossing as the zero. On the .308 test profile with an 8 in target, that is **249 yd rather than 21 yd**.

This follows the proposal in the issue, but people now sight in at the far distance, which may be harder in practice. **The owner should confirm this choice.**

The page still shows the near zero, 21 yd in this example. Zeros already saved in profiles do not change.

## How to use

1. Open `/calculators/mpbr` with an active profile.
2. Enter the target size in in or cm, then calculate.
3. Read the optimal, near and far zero, the point blank range and the chart.
4. "Set MPBR for current profile" saves the optimal zero to the profile.

The page, form and translations did not change.

## Technical changes

### Search

All in `calculateMPBR` in [`src/lib/utils/ballisticCalculator.js`](../src/lib/utils/ballisticCalculator.js):

- It bisects barrel elevation until the apex touches the top of the zone.
- It takes the far crossing of that arc, down to a whole unit, as the zero.
- Everything returned describes the shot zeroed at that value, so `optimalZero` equals `farZero`.
- Distances are interpolated between step 1 rows, so they no longer sit on a 5 unit grid.
- `MPBR_MAX_RANGE` (20000) bounds the flight. A shot that reaches it gives null, never a capped number.

### Guards

The search only fires a shot that passes `hasFlyableVelocity`, has a finite sight height, a BC of at least 0.01 and a velocity under 10000 fps. The old code rejected only a BC of 0 or less.

It also gives null when:

- the flight ends inside the zone
- the arc cannot reach the top of the zone within 0.5 rad
- the zone is too thin for any whole unit zero
- the target is infinite

Slow loads that used to give null can now return a result.

### Dope cards

[`src/lib/utils/dopeCardZeros.js`](../src/lib/utils/dopeCardZeros.js) is new and adds `findZeroCrossings`. [`dopeCardImperial.js`](../src/lib/utils/dopeCardImperial.js) and [`dopeCardMetric.js`](../src/lib/utils/dopeCardMetric.js) now read near and far from one extra calculation to 3000 units. They used to print `zeroDist` as the near zero, which would have labelled a saved far zero as near.

- A crossing past 3000 units stays blank.
- A bullet with no near crossing leaves near blank.
- `isFlyableZeroElevation` skips the extra calculation for NaN or an elevation over 0.6 rad, because the engine can hang on such a shot. Near and far are then blank.

### Speed

Typical targets are about 10 times faster. Only targets of hundreds of feet are slower.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Normal targets, measured during development, in Node | about 370 to 530 ms | about 31 to 59 ms |
| 8 to 30 in, rerun for this document, in the dev container | 546 to 631 ms | 49 to 80 ms |
| 300 in, rerun for this document | 552 to 577 ms | 120 to 147 ms |

The rerun used one call per case on the .223, .308 and 6.5 test profiles.

The bound is 54 fires plus two zero solves, 174 integrations in all. The worst case measured during development was about 4.6 s on an absurd input. There is no time budget, because the calculation runs only from a button.

## Security review

**PASS.** The result of round 3 is added on [#86](https://github.com/hawkins48b/cw-zro/issues/86).

Findings fixed during the review:

- dope cards mislabelling the saved zero
- an understated cost claim
- a new hang in the extra card calculation

Pre-existing, not fixed:

- Absurd or tampered sight heights can hang the engine.
- Saved zeros can now be much larger than before, up to about 16700, which slows recomputes on extreme values.

## Known limitations / follow-ups

- **Owner decision.** Confirm that saving the far crossing is the right choice for "Set MPBR".
- **Not verified.** The chart in a browser and timings on a phone.
- **Rendered PDF cards not viewed.** Field values and coordinates were checked, but no one has viewed a rendered page.
- **Atmosphere and powder.** `calculateMPBR` still ignores powder sensitivity and uses a standard atmosphere.
